Rules

This page describes the default rules. You can switch flying with three stones, a random first player and a time per turn in the app.

Goal

The aim is to take so many of your opponent's stones that fewer than three remain, or to block them so they cannot move.

Board and stones

The game is played on 24 points arranged as three nested squares and joined by lines. Each player has 9 stones. Three of your own stones in a row along a line form a mill.

Placing

Taking turns, each player places their 9 stones on any empty points. All stones are placed first; only then do they move.

Moving

Once all stones are placed, players take turns moving one stone along a line to the next empty point.

Mill

Whoever closes a mill takes one of the opponent's stones off the board. Stones in a closed mill are protected unless there are no others. A mill may be opened and closed again and counts anew each time.

Flying

A player with only three stones left may jump with a stone to any empty point. This rule is switched on in the settings.

End of the game

You lose when you have fewer than three stones or cannot move. The game is a draw when the same position occurs 3 times or 100 moves in a row (both players together) pass without a mill.

Time per turn

You have 60 seconds per turn. When time runs out, a legal move is played for you.

Computer

The computer sees only the board, just like you. On Beginner it often makes mistakes; on Expert it looks furthest ahead.

Nine Men’s Morris is a game by Robert Peringer.
